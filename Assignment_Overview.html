3. Problem and dataset

Land is the largest purchase most Sri Lankan families make, yet there's no public, plot-level price index for the Colombo district. Buyers and sellers judge prices from a few advertisements, and asking prices for similar land can differ several times over. Goal: estimate a fair asking price per perch from a plot's area, size, land type and surroundings.

Dataset

  • Source: Land prices of Colombo district, Kaggle (ruchiraayeshmantha)
  • Size: 4,494 rows, 35 columns, no missing values
  • Period: listings posted 31 Jul - 21 Sep 2021
  • Coverage: 160 areas; busiest are Ranala (350), Makandana (270), Kaduwela (250)

Columns

GroupColumns
ListingAddress (area), land size (perches), land type, posting date
PriceMentioned price, price scale, price per perch
Location (27)Distance to Colombo Fort; distance to and number of nearby schools, universities, hospitals, banks, finance companies, supermarkets, fuel stations, railway, expressway

Key finding: all 27 location features have exactly one value per area. They were computed from the area name, not each plot's position, so the dataset has only 160 distinct location "points". Within an area, only land size, land type and price differ.

5. Exploratory data analysis

  • Area dominates price: median price per perch ranges from about Rs 215,000 (Kosgama) to Rs 18 million (Kurunduwatta), an 84x difference. All the most expensive areas are in central Colombo.
  • Many rare areas: median of 8 listings per area; 88 of 160 areas have fewer than 10.
  • Strongest relationships (Spearman): distance from Fort ρ = −0.81, distance to grade-A school −0.78, supermarkets within 2 km +0.76, banks within 2 km +0.75, land size +0.40.
  • Multicollinearity: 22 of 45 pairs among the top-10 features have |ρ| > 0.7. The location features mostly describe one thing: how central and built-up an area is.

6. Methodology (02_modelling.ipynb)

Split and validation

  • One listing (Dampe, 9.7 perches) set aside first for the inference demo.
  • 80/20 split, stratified by price thirds: 3,419 train / 855 test.
  • All tuning and model selection with 5-fold cross-validation on training data only.
  • Metric: RMSE of log price (0.37 ≈ predictions within about 1.45x), plus R², and MAE / MAPE / median % error in rupees.

Models

Ridge regressionBaseline; the one model that needs scaling
Random ForestRequired; averages many de-correlated trees, robust to noise

12. Limitations and future work

  • Asking prices, not sale prices. Real transaction prices are usually lower and aren't public.
  • 2021 data. The 2022 economic crisis changed prices; relative patterns are more likely to hold than rupee values.
  • Location per area, not per plot. The biggest limitation: a main-road plot and a narrow-lane plot in the same area look identical.
  • Missing plot features: road frontage and width, shape, slope, flood risk.
  • Repeated developer listings can appear in both train and test, making seen-area scores somewhat optimistic.

14. Viva preparation: likely questions

Why price per perch and not total price?

It makes plots of different sizes comparable, and it's how land is advertised in Sri Lanka. Mentioned price was dropped because it contains the target (leakage).

Why log-transform the target?

Price per perch is extremely right-skewed (skew 7.19). After log1p the skew is 0.22, models fit better, and errors become proportional (an RMSE of 0.37 means predictions are typically within about 1.45x of the true price).

Why IQR on the log price instead of the raw price?

Plain IQR would remove 297 listings, mostly genuine expensive land in central Colombo. On the log scale only 21 implausible values are flagged.

Why target encoding for Address?

160 areas, 88 of them with fewer than 10 listings. One-hot encoding would create 160 sparse columns. Cross-fitted target encoding never uses a listing's own price to encode it.

Why not default hyperparameters?

Defaults overfit this noisy data (unlimited RF depth, fast XGBoost learning rate). Tuning cut CV RMSE from about 0.445 to 0.429 and raised test R² from about 0.83 to 0.86.

Why was stacking chosen if all models are so close?

It had the lowest cross-validated RMSE (0.4265), and selection was done on CV, not the test set. The near-tie itself is a finding: accuracy is limited by the data, not the algorithm.

What is the most important finding?

All 27 location features have one value per area, so 81% of price variation is between areas. SHAP confirms location drives 87% of the model's decisions.

How do SHAP and LIME differ?

SHAP splits each prediction into exact feature contributions that add up to the prediction. LIME fits a simple linear model around one listing. They agreed on the top three drivers for the held-out listing.

Why does the app only offer known areas?

For unseen areas, R² falls from about 0.83 to about 0.69 (grouped cross-validation), so predictions for unknown areas would be much less reliable.

What would improve the model most?

Better data: per-plot coordinates, recent (post-2022) prices, actual sale prices, and plot features such as road access and frontage.
