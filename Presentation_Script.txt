PRESENTATION SCRIPT - Predicting Land Prices in the Colombo District
IT5514 Applied Machine Learning - about 5 minutes
=====================================================================


SLIDE 1 - Title  (0:00 - 0:25)
-------------------------------
This project predicts land prices in the Colombo district.
Land is the biggest purchase most Sri Lankan families make, but there is no
public, plot-level price guide. People judge prices from a few online ads,
and similar plots are advertised at very different prices. So I built a model
that estimates a fair asking price per perch from a plot's area, size and land
type, and put it in a live web app.


SLIDE 2 - Data and preparation  (0:25 - 1:05)
---------------------------------------------
The data is 4,494 real land advertisements from the Colombo district, posted
in 2021. I chose price per perch as the target, because it makes plots of
different sizes comparable, and I dropped the advertised total price because it
would leak the answer. Three listings were priced per acre and never converted,
so I fixed them, and I removed duplicates. And because prices are extremely
skewed, removing outliers directly would have thrown away 297 genuine expensive
plots in central Colombo. So I log-transformed the price first, which left only
21 real outliers. That gave 4,275 clean listings.


SLIDE 3 - What the data says  (1:05 - 1:35)
-------------------------------------------
The exploratory analysis showed one thing very clearly: location sets the
price. The median price per perch differs 84 times between the cheapest area,
Kosgama, and the most expensive, Kurunduwatta in central Colombo. Distance from
Colombo Fort has a correlation of minus 0.81 with price. And the location
features are strongly correlated with each other. They mostly describe one
thing: how central and built-up an area is.


SLIDE 4 - Model accuracy  (1:35 - 2:30)
---------------------------------------
This table compares every model I trained. Random Forest and XGBoost were
taught in the module, so together with a Ridge baseline they are my comparison
models. My new algorithm is CatBoost, which was not covered in the lectures. I
chose it because the area column has 160 categories, many of them rare, and
CatBoost encodes categories without leaking the target. It reached an R-squared
of 0.866, tied for best, and it generalised best to areas it had never seen.

Tuning with five-fold cross-validation clearly helped: about 0.83 with default
settings, 0.86 tuned. The final model is a stacking ensemble of Random Forest,
XGBoost and CatBoost, chosen because it had the lowest cross-validated error.
It reaches a median error of about 12 percent.


SLIDE 5 - Key finding  (2:30 - 3:10)
------------------------------------
The most important finding came from the data itself. All 27 location features
have exactly one value per area. They were calculated from the area name, not
from each plot. So 81 percent of price variation is between areas. When I
tested the models on areas they had never seen, accuracy dropped, but that's
where the tree models clearly beat the linear model: R-squared 0.70 against
0.54. Because of this, the app only offers areas the model knows.


SLIDE 6 - Explainability: SHAP and LIME  (3:10 - 3:50)
------------------------------------------------------
To explain the model I used SHAP and LIME on the final ensemble. SHAP shows that
location drives 87 percent of the model's decisions, land type 9 and land size
4. For a single held-out plot in Dampe, the waterfall shows why it was priced
low: it's 28 kilometres from Fort, Dampe is a low-priced area, and the nearest
grade-A school is far away. LIME, which works in a completely different way,
agreed on the same top three drivers, and CatBoost's own exact SHAP values show
the same 87 percent location share.


SLIDE 7 - Live demo  (3:50 - 4:35)
----------------------------------
Here is the deployed application.
   >> Alt+Tab to the browser. Select "battaramulla", 10 perches, Residential,
      click "Estimate price".
I choose an area and a size. Battaramulla, about 11 kilometres from Fort, comes
out at about 2.6 million rupees per perch, in the high band.
   >> Select "kaduwela", click "Estimate price".
Kaduwela, further out, comes out at about 460,000, in the low band.
   >> Scroll down to "Why this price?".
Under each price, the app explains it: for Battaramulla, its location roughly
doubles the price compared with a typical plot. Behind it is a GitHub
repository with 15 automated tests, a retraining pipeline with a quality gate,
and automatic redeployment.
   >> Alt+Tab back to the slides.


SLIDE 8 - Conclusion  (4:35 - 4:55)
-----------------------------------
To conclude: all tuned models perform almost the same, so the data, not the
algorithm, limits the accuracy. The main limitations are asking prices rather
than sale prices, 2021 data, and location recorded per area. Per-plot location
data would improve the model most.

LIKELY QUESTIONS
  "What is your new algorithm, and how is it different?"
    "CatBoost. Unlike a decision tree or Random Forest, it handles categories
     itself with ordered target statistics, so the area name is encoded without
     leaking the price; it also uses ordered boosting and symmetric trees, which
     reduce overfitting on small, noisy data."
  "Why stacking and not CatBoost?"
    "I selected on cross-validation, not the test set, and stacking had the lowest
     cross-validated error, 0.4265 against 0.4311 for CatBoost."
